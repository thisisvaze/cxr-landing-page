# CuriosityXR learning API: first sales test

Research checked September 27, 2026. Owner: Aaditya Vaze. Requests: **vaze@curiosityxr.com**.

## Recommendation

Start with edtech founders and developers building science tutors or lesson-authoring products. Sell a small, reviewed content pilot for their own topics. The proposed product is a learning package: an educational response with useful images, labeled diagrams, and a 3D asset where appropriate. The buyer gets a component they can integrate into their existing product.

There is credible adjacent demand for AI-assisted learning content and visual learning. **Demand for this particular bundle, at a sustainable price, remains unvalidated.** A paid pilot and repeat purchase are better evidence than a waitlist, general AI adoption, or compliments about the demo.

## Markets and buyers

These priorities are judgments based on the product fit and your stated access to edtech founders, not measured segment conversion rates.

| Priority | Buyer and trigger | Initial offer | Why this order |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Founder/CTO of a science tutor or lesson tool; adding visual explanations this quarter | One learner level, one subject, a small topic batch | Reachable decision maker, concrete integration, repeat content need to test |
| 2 | Product lead at an AI authoring platform; teachers/trainers need better subject visuals | Diagrams and explanations inside an existing lesson workflow | Clear workflow, but many platforms already generate images |

| Later | Direct schools, universities, large publishers, museums | Institutional or catalog licensing | Requires a separate buyer, procurement, and delivery model; explore after founder sales |

Start English-only with warm Canadian connections and US edtech founders. Add UK/English-speaking teams when introductions arise. This is a practical language, time-zone, and access choice—not evidence that one country has the largest market. Avoid building localization or curriculum coverage before a buyer requires it.

## Offer to test privately

**Starting hypothesis: US$500 for a two-week pilot covering up to 20 agreed topic packages**, one learner level, one revision pass, and one integration review. Validate the cost of a representative sample before quoting. Reduce the topic count or revise the quote if review, modeling, or licensing would make the work uneconomic. This is not public pricing or a delivery promise.

Provide one free representative sample after qualification. Confirm required formats per topic; do not promise four assets for every question. Use a curated, reviewed 3D asset only when it helps and the intended use is permitted. Custom modeling and specialist validation need separate scope. Agree delivery, acceptance, payment, and remedies in writing before paid work; use your existing invoicing/payment process.

The first content evaluation can be delivered manually with a documented package structure. Call it a **content pilot** until a usable API integration exists. Do not imply a manual batch is a working production API.

Suggested acceptance agreement:

- Buyer supplies 20 representative topics, learner level, intended use, and required formats before work begins.
- Buyer reviews accuracy, age suitability, visual relevance, readable labels, and usefulness against their current workflow.
- Each accepted package has the required deliverables and source/asset permission information. Missing or failed items are explicit.
- Target at least 16 of 20 packages accepted after the agreed revision, with every delivered package reviewed. Unaccepted items stay out of learner use. Measure authoring/review time against the buyer’s baseline; set the time-saving target with them.
- At the end, ask for a paid next batch or a scoped integration commitment. Do not claim improved learning outcomes from a content review alone.

Record generation, retries, review labor, licensing, storage, and delivery cost per **accepted** package. For a chosen contribution margin, quote at least `total delivery cost / (1 - target margin)`. For example, $150 of measured delivery cost at a 70% target margin implies a $500 price. Those are illustrative assumptions, not measured CuriosityXR costs. Set usage pricing only after real pilot usage and costs are known.

## A pipeline you can run from your inbox

Use **vaze@curiosityxr.com + the CSV tracker + your existing calendar/invoice tools**. No CRM required yet.

Stages: `Research → Contacted → Discovery → Sample → Pilot proposed → Paid pilot → Repeat`, plus `Closed / not now`. Keep one next action and a date for every active lead. Log source, current workaround, example topics, budget, integration owner, and reason lost. Populate actual contact details privately after verification; do not commit a live customer/contact list to the repository.

The website form sends each request to vaze@curiosityxr.com via Resend (`/api/access-request`, needs `RESEND_API_KEY` and a verified curiosityxr.com sender); reply-to is the visitor. Nothing is stored, so the inbox is the record; count only **received emails** as inbound requests. Reply personally within one business day as an operating target, then log the lead and proposed next step. Add durable storage when traffic justifies it.

| When | Your action | Decision/evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–2 | Pick one subject, cost and review a real sample, assemble 30 accounts (ten warm if available), deploy the reviewed page when ready | A demonstrable output and clear delivery limits |
| Days 3–5 | Start ten individual conversations through warm introductions or appropriate business/community channels | Aim for five discovery calls; record actual problems and existing alternatives |
| Days 6–8 | Ask qualified teams for representative topics; prepare up to three samples; approach remaining relevant accounts | Do they spend time reviewing and provide an integration owner? |
| Days 9–12 | Review samples together and propose a bounded paid pilot | Ask for payment/commitment, not just an expression of interest |
| Days 13–14 | Follow up once where appropriate, update reasons lost, review funnel | Aim for two paid pilots; this is an experiment target, not a forecast |
| After pilot delivery | Compare accepted content and time/cost with baseline; request a paid next step | Repeat purchase and viable delivery economics justify further investment |

If 30 well-matched approaches produce fewer than five useful conversations, first revisit targeting, channel, and message. If five or more qualified discussions produce no willingness to pay after sample review, investigate the gap before broadening development. If two teams pay but none renew or the work is excessively custom, revise scope and economics before adding a public self-serve API. These small-sample thresholds guide decisions; they do not establish market size statistically.

## Messages to use yourself

Warm introduction / individual message:

> Hi [Name] — I’m building CuriosityXR’s learning content API. I saw [specific product/workflow]. How do you currently source diagrams and visual explanations for science topics? I’m testing small packages that combine an explanation with relevant visuals and, where useful, a 3D model. If that’s a problem you’re working on, could we review one of your real topics together for 15 minutes?

After they share a brief:

> Thanks. I’ll use [topic] for [learner level] and focus on [formats]. Before preparing it, what would make the result useful enough to replace part of your current workflow, and who would review it? If the sample works, we can scope a paid batch with clear acceptance criteria.

After a successful sample review:

> Based on the sample, I propose [topic count] packages for [price] over [agreed dates], with [formats], one revision pass, and [delivery/integration scope]. We’ll evaluate [criteria]. Would this solve a current priority, and who can approve the pilot?

Discovery questions: What did the last visual lesson cost in time/money? Which outputs need the most correction? What do you already use? Which five real topics should we test? Who owns integration, content approval, and budget? Is batch delivery enough initially? What would prevent commercial use? What would make you buy the next batch?

## Product work required before promising production access

The inspected `cxr-content-api/README.md` describes private generation routes and quality outcomes; `curiosityxr-webapp-service/api/access/routes.py` covers existing app access. Those pieces are useful foundations, but this review did not establish a working external, four-format package API. The website example is labeled illustrative. The launch page and email flow do not issue API keys or generate content.

Keep the next build tied to the first pilot:

1. Produce a repeatable reviewed sample for one subject. Track source, creator, license, attribution, quality status, and omissions per asset.
2. Agree a small package contract: topic/question, learner level, objective, requested formats in; educational response, assets, metadata, and explicit missing/review states out. Confirm whether delivery can be batch/asynchronous before promising real-time responses.
3. For an external API pilot, add scoped customer authentication, tenant isolation, request limits/cost caps, reliable result retrieval, and a measured integration example using the existing services. Keep private generation services private.
4. Publish real samples, supported coverage, measured delivery expectations, and pricing only when demonstrated. Add automated billing, a dashboard, and SDKs when repeated customer demand warrants them.

**Asset supply is a material dependency.** Sketchfab’s developer terms require end-user authentication for the Download API unless a specific agreement says otherwise, plus license display and creator attribution. A searchable catalog does not establish rights to resell/rehost all of its assets. Verify the selected asset and delivery rights for each pilot; use owned or explicitly licensed alternatives where needed. Do not sell “1M+ commercially redistributable models” on the strength of catalog size. [Sketchfab API terms, sections 4.5–4.7](https://sketchfab.com/developers/terms)

The immediate goal is two customers paying for a useful, repeatable package and evidence that at least one wants more. Build the public API around that demonstrated workflow.
